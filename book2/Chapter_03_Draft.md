# Book 2 · Chapter 3 — DRAFT

**CHAPTER 3**
## A Sky Full of Stars

Years passed. Abram had moved to Canaan, built altars, and trusted God. But he and Sarai still had no children.

Then the word of the Lord came to Abram in a vision:

> "Do not be afraid, Abram. I am your shield, your very great reward." —Genesis 15:1

Abram was honest with God. He said that he still had no child, and that a servant in his household would one day receive everything he owned.

Joesiah nodded slowly. "He told God how he really felt."

"He did," Grandma said. "You can bring God your honest questions."

Then God took Abram outside, under the night sky.

> "Look up at the sky and count the stars—if indeed you can count them… So shall your offspring be." —Genesis 15:5

Malani leaned back and pointed upward. "One… two… three… four… TOO MANY!"

Everyone laughed.

"That's exactly the point," Grandma said. "Abram couldn't count them. And God's promise was bigger than anything Abram could count."

Scripture says:

> "Abram believed the LORD, and he credited it to him as righteousness." —Genesis 15:6

Many more years went by. When Abram was ninety-nine years old, God appeared to him again and confirmed His covenant, a sacred promise. God gave Abram a new name: **Abraham**, which means "father of many."

> "No longer will you be called Abram; your name will be Abraham, for I have made you a father of many nations." —Genesis 17:5

God also changed Sarai's name to **Sarah** and promised that she would have a son. God even told them the baby's name: **Isaac**.

Joesiah's mouth dropped open. "God named the baby before he was born?"

"Before he was born," Grandma said. "When God makes a promise, He already knows how it ends."

*God keeps His promises, even when it takes generations.*

**READ IT FOR YOURSELF:** Genesis 15:1–6 and Genesis 17:1–8, 15–19

### Notes for you (not part of the book)
- **Length:** about 360 words.
- **Scripture:** all quotations are NIV (Genesis 15:1, 15:5, 15:6 and 17:5). Abram's honest question paraphrases Genesis 15:2–3. His age and the new names follow Genesis 17:1, 15 and 19.
- **"Father of many":** I wrote "father of many" rather than "father of many nations" as a definition of the name. That's the closest literal meaning, and the verse quoted underneath gives the full "father of many nations."
- **Timeline:** Hagar's story (Genesis 16) actually happens between Genesis 15 and 17. This chapter says "many more years went by" to leave room for it, and Chapter 4 goes back to tell it.
- **Illustration idea:** Abram outside his tent under a sky blazing with stars, with Malani counting on her fingers beside Grandma and Joesiah.
