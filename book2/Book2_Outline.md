# Book 2 Outline: *Joesiah & Malani: From Abraham to Moses — The Journey to Freedom*

**Series:** Joesiah & Malani, Book 2 · **Author:** LeTisha C. Cleveland · **Ages:** 8–12
**Format:** same as Book 1. Each chapter is one page of text facing one full-page illustration (14 chapters, about 32 pages). Most chapters end with a **READ IT FOR YOURSELF** line.

This is a **draft outline for your approval**. Change, cut or reorder anything.

---

## How Book 2 connects to Book 1

- Book 1 ended with Joseph's family in Egypt, a new Pharaoh, and "a baby would be born… His name was Moses." Its last page promised Book 2 with five stops: **Abraham (a covenant begins) → Isaac (the promise continues) → Jacob (a family grows) → Egypt and Israel (a nation is formed) → Moses (a people are freed).** This outline follows those five stops in order.
- **Opening:** Grandma explains that to understand the freedom story, they first go *back* to the promise God made to Abraham.
- **Joseph:** Book 1 already told Joseph's story, so Book 2 recaps it in one short scene (Chapter 8) instead of retelling it.

## Recurring features (carried over from Book 1)

- Grandma, Joesiah and Malani travel through the story together. Joesiah asks the questions; Malani adds short, joyful lines.
- **Scripture vs. history.** Grandma keeps separating what the Bible says from what historians and archaeologists debate.
- **The motto** appears 3–4 times: *Know the Word. Seek the Truth. Remember who you are. Walk in the purpose God gave you.*
- **"I WILL READ BEFORE I REPEAT."** returns at least once.
- **New repeating thread:** *"God keeps His promises, even when it takes generations."*

## Part One: A Covenant Begins (Abraham)

### Chapter 1: Back to the Promise
- **Story:** Grandma opens the family tree again. Through Shem's line came Terah, and Terah's son Abram lived in Ur and later Haran.
- **Lesson:** Every freedom story begins with a promise.
- **Read it for yourself:** Genesis 11:10–32
- **Illustration idea:** Grandma, Joesiah and Malani at the family-tree scroll, with a glowing path leading from Shem to Abram.

### Chapter 2: Abram Answers the Call
- **Story:** God tells Abram to leave his country, and promises a great nation and a blessing for "all peoples on earth." Abram obeys without knowing where he's going.
- **Lesson:** Faith means taking the next step before you see the whole road.
- **Read it for yourself:** Genesis 12:1–9
- **Illustration idea:** Abram and Sarai's caravan setting out, with the children walking alongside.

### Chapter 3: A Sky Full of Stars
- **Story:** God tells Abram to count the stars: "So shall your offspring be." Abram believes God, and his name becomes **Abraham**, "father of many nations."
- **Lesson:** God's promises are bigger than what we can count.
- **Read it for yourself:** Genesis 15:1–6 and Genesis 17:1–8
- **Illustration idea:** A night sky over the desert, with Malani trying to count the stars.

### Chapter 4: Hagar — The God Who Sees
- **Story:** Hagar was an **Egyptian** servant. She ran into the wilderness, and God met her there. She named Him **El Roi**, "the God who sees me." Later, God cares for Hagar and her son **Ishmael**.
- **Lesson:** God sees people others overlook.
- **Read it for yourself:** Genesis 16 and Genesis 21:8–21
- **Illustration idea:** Hagar by a spring in the desert, with an angel's light.
- **Why it matters for the series:** it continues Book 1's theme that Africa and Egypt are part of the Bible's story from the beginning.

## Part Two: The Promise Continues (Isaac)

### Chapter 5: Isaac, the Son of Laughter
- **Story:** Isaac is born when Abraham and Sarah are very old, and Sarah laughs with joy. Then comes the hard test on Mount Moriah, and God provides a ram.
- **Lesson:** God provides.
- **Read it for yourself:** Genesis 21:1–7 and Genesis 22:1–14
- **Writing note:** tell Genesis 22 gently for ages 8–12. Focus on trust and "God will provide", not on danger.
- **Illustration idea:** Baby Isaac with laughing parents; the ram caught in the thicket.

### Chapter 6: Rebekah and the Twins
- **Story:** Rebekah shows kindness at the well. Later her twin sons **Esau and Jacob** are born, and the brothers struggle with each other.
- **Lesson:** Kindness matters, and so does honesty in a family.
- **Read it for yourself:** Genesis 24 and Genesis 25:19–34
- **Illustration idea:** Rebekah drawing water for the camels.

## Part Three: A Family Grows (Jacob)

### Chapter 7: A Ladder to Heaven and a New Name
- **Story:** Jacob dreams of a stairway to heaven, and God repeats the promise He made to Abraham. Years later Jacob wrestles through the night, and God renames him **Israel**.
- **Lesson:** God can change our name, our story and our heart.
- **Read it for yourself:** Genesis 28:10–22 and Genesis 32:22–30
- **Illustration idea:** Jacob asleep on a stone, with a glowing stairway above him.

### Chapter 8: Twelve Sons, Twelve Tribes
- **Story:** Jacob's twelve sons become the **twelve tribes of Israel**.
- **Book 1 recap, in one short scene:** Malani shouts "Joseph!" and Grandma recaps how Joseph rose in Egypt and saved his family.
- **New detail:** Joseph married **Asenath**, an Egyptian woman. Their sons **Ephraim and Manasseh** were blessed by Jacob and became tribes of Israel too.
- **Lesson:** God's family has always included many peoples.
- **Read it for yourself:** Genesis 35:22–26, Genesis 41:50–52 and Genesis 48
- **Illustration idea:** Jacob blessing his grandsons.

## Part Four: A Nation Is Formed (Egypt and Israel)

### Chapter 9: A New King Who Did Not Know Joseph
- **Story:** Israel grows into a nation. A fearful new Pharaoh forces them into slavery. The midwives **Shiphrah and Puah** fear God and refuse to obey his cruel order.
- **Lesson:** Courage can come from people the powerful ignore.
- **Read it for yourself:** Exodus 1
- **Important framing:** Grandma says the oppression came from a ruler's fear and cruelty, not from the Egyptian people as a whole. This protects Book 1's message about Egypt's great civilization.
- **Illustration idea:** The midwives standing firm.

### Chapter 10: A Baby in a Basket
- **Story:** Moses' mother **Jochebed** hides her baby in a basket on the Nile. His sister **Miriam** watches over him. **Pharaoh's daughter**, an Egyptian princess, rescues and adopts him.
- **Lesson:** God uses mothers, sisters and even unexpected strangers.
- **Read it for yourself:** Exodus 2:1–10
- **Illustration idea:** Miriam hiding in the reeds as the princess finds the basket.

## Part Five: A People Are Freed (Moses)

### Chapter 11: The Burning Bush
- **Story:** Moses flees to Midian, where he marries **Zipporah**, daughter of **Jethro**. At a bush that burns but isn't consumed, God says, "I AM WHO I AM," and sends Moses back to free His people. Moses worries he isn't a good speaker, and God gives him his brother **Aaron** to help.
- **Lesson:** God calls people who feel unready.
- **Read it for yourself:** Exodus 2:11–4:17
- **Illustration idea:** Moses barefoot before the burning bush.

### Chapter 12: "Let My People Go"
- **Story:** Moses and Aaron go before Pharaoh. The plagues come, and then the first **Passover**.
- **Lesson:** No ruler is greater than God, and God remembers the oppressed.
- **Read it for yourself:** Exodus 5–12
- **Writing note:** handle the death of the firstborn gently and briefly, focusing on the Passover as protection and remembrance.
- **Illustration idea:** A family at the Passover meal, with lamb's blood on the doorframe.

### Chapter 13: A Path Through the Sea
- **Story:** God parts the Red Sea, and Israel crosses on dry ground. **Miriam** leads the women in a song of praise with tambourines. Exodus also says a **"mixed multitude"** (other peoples) left Egypt with them.
- **Lesson:** Freedom is something to celebrate and to remember.
- **Read it for yourself:** Exodus 12:37–38, Exodus 14 and Exodus 15:19–21
- **History note:** the location of the crossing and the date of the Exodus are debated by scholars. Grandma explains this in one sentence.
- **Illustration idea:** Miriam dancing with a tambourine, with walls of water behind her. *Malani's favorite page. This is the Book 2 cover scene.*

### Chapter 14: Freedom With a Purpose
- **Story:** At Mount Sinai, God gives the **Ten Commandments**: freedom with a way to live. Numbers 12:1 tells us Moses had a **Cushite wife**; Cush is the land the children learned about in Book 1. Grandma ties together all five stops of the journey and the promise kept across generations.
- **Lesson:** Freed people are called to live free, love God and love others.
- **Read it for yourself:** Exodus 19–20 and Numbers 12:1
- **Ending:** "Our History Continues…", a **Book 3 teaser**. Title to be decided. For example, a journey that leads to **Yeshua**.
